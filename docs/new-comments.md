# New-comments marker (`new_comments.js`)

Read this before touching `new_comments.js`, `screen_annotations.applyNewCommentsMark` or the `.newCommentsStart` CSS.

## What it is

When the reader reopens an article they have read, a thin neutral-grey line is drawn along the top edge of the first comment that appeared after their last finished reading, with a line of small text on the right, 「以下是新推文」 ("new comments below"). The floating tool panel (the 「⋯」 at the bottom right) gains a 「跳到新推文」 ("jump to new comments") button, which scrolls only the easy-reading viewport. The switch is `markNewComments` (on by default; it sends no bytes at all, so it is not on the native safe mode list).

## Rules

- **Sends no bytes at all**: the comment count comes from easy reading's accumulated page (`buf.pageLines`); the article's identity is board plus AID (`aid_navigation.findLocalPostAid`, which only reads the URL line at the article's end and never presses `Q`). Records live in localStorage `pttchrome.newComments.v1` (`{ "<board, lower case>/<AID>": [comment count, time] }`, at most `NEW_COMMENTS_LIMIT` articles, dropping the oldest beyond that); nothing goes to prefs or the cloud.
- **Recorded only when easy reading reaches the article's end** (`easyReadingReachedPageEnd`, not in functionMode, not during reverse reading): an article not read to its end does not count as seen. What is recorded is the comment count at that moment; the marker for the current reading stays where it was placed when the article opened (`_seen` does not change for the whole reading). Two cases come from the page stack's live layers (`docs/article-swipe-drawer-spec.md`):
  - **A revealed source holds the reading** ([#121](https://github.com/NoReasonKen/PttChrome/issues/121)): when Back reveals an article that was read to its end, its live layer keeps that reading's rows and marker state, and PTT's reopened copy waits behind it. While the handoff's layer holds the reading (`StackArticleLayer.holdsReading()`, true while the layer kept that content), `_onSettled` records nothing: the article on screen is the layer's, and the comment count of PTT's reopened copy, which may include comments the reader has not seen, is not recorded as read.
  - **Comments appended behind the marker** ([#126](https://github.com/NoReasonKen/PttChrome/issues/126)): when the real article takes over from that layer (`StackArticleLayer._adopt`), it first restores the layer's marker state. If PTT's reopened copy still starts with the layer's content, the comments it gained are appended at the end and `appended(lines, shownRows)` runs: the comments in the first `shownRows` rows (the layer's rows, which the reader saw) become `_seen`, so the marker moves to the first appended comment, and `lines`, read to its end, is recorded as read the way a settle records it (when `markNewComments` or `listNewPushCount` is on). A copy that no longer starts with the layer's content, as after an edit or a deleted comment, appends nothing, and `appended` is not called: the layer's rows stay shown with 「文章已更新」 ("the article has been updated"), while the accumulation state keeps describing PTT's copy (`term_view._showsOverCopy`). The next page PTT sends, as after a comment, rebuilds the article from PTT's copy first and then accumulates as usual, so nothing is spliced onto rows PTT no longer has. A source read only partly ([#121](https://github.com/NoReasonKen/PttChrome/issues/121)) also takes the layer's rows and marker state, and easy reading pages PTT's copy on from where that read stopped; `appended` is not called, since the rows past that read were never seen, so the marker stays where the reading had it, and `readToEnd(lines)` records the count of the article read to its end the way a settle does.
- **Comments are counted the way floors are counted**: `CommentScanner` uses `comment_parse.FloorCounter` (the BePTT algorithm) and only counts comments after the 「※ 發信站／文章網址」 (posting site / article URL) line, so fake comments inside the body do not count, and what is recorded matches what is looked up. New-format comments (`│` content rows) are not counted yet, so such articles are not marked (unrecognised means hands off).
- **Reverse reading (End)**: new pages are inserted in the middle and row numbers shift, so the scan is discarded and nothing is marked or recorded; once the join completes, the article is rescanned from the top.
- **Annotation layer**: term_view calls `newComments.rowFor(lines)` only while drawing easy reading's accumulated page (`stableRows` and `lines === buf.pageLines`), and puts the result in `enhance.newCommentsRow` (part of `annotationsKey`; within one article it flips from null to a row at most once, so that frame recomputes everything). `applyNewCommentsMark` writes only `result[row]`: a row merged into a same-author comment block marks the block's first row, and a row hidden by the blacklist moves forward to the first visible comment. Afterwards the frontier fast path reuses a prefix of objects that are already marked, and rows past the frontier are re-marked every frame, so `annotationFrontier` and `annotationsAreRowIndependent` need no change.
- **Rendering**: `row.js` and `comment_card.js` add class `newCommentsStart` and `data-new-comments` (the small text, i18n `new_comments_divider`) to that row; the CSS makes room with the row's `margin-top` and draws the line plus the small text with `::before` (not selectable or copyable). Golden snapshots are unaffected (rows without a mark render unchanged).
- **Design**: neutral grey, no colour (the comment area already has OP blue, own-comment green and floor yellow, and L asked for the comment area not to get gaudy). The small text must stay legible: font size at least 0.7 of the row's, light grey on black at a contrast of 7:1 or more, and the whole line inside the row's `margin-top` gap. The comment spacing rule (`.commentSpacing`) sets `margin-top` with a more specific selector, so `.newCommentsStart` needs an equally specific selector that overrides it; otherwise the gap is reset to 0.55em and the small text overlaps the row above (guarded by offline e2e).

## +N on the article list (pref `listNewPushCount`, on by default)

For an article the reader has finished, a small 「+N」 box appears beside its comment count on the article list. It also sends no bytes and is not on the native safe mode list.

- **The list's number is not a comment count**: pttbbs `post.c` changes the `.DIR` recommend field by +1 for a push, -1 for a boo and nothing for an arrow (clamped to ±100); `bbs.c#readdoent` draws the number only for 1 to 99, 「爆」 for 100 and above, a blank for 0 and -1 to -10, and 「X…」 below that. So reading to the end also records the article's **recommendation score** (`CommentScanner.score`, counted like the floors, only after the meta line). N = the list's number − the score at the finished reading, shown only when > 0. When the list shows 爆, a blank, X or a locked article, nothing is shown (not exact).
- **The list has no AID**: the record also stores the author (from the header, lower case) and the subject (`long_push_anchor.subjectKey`, with Re: and Fw: removed). A list row is compared with `listRowIdentity` (titles truncated with … still match), and counts only when it matches **exactly one** record on that board. Several read articles with the same author and subject, reposts (whose header carries the original author) and articles whose title was changed all fail to match, so nothing is shown. Old-format records (comment count only) take no part.
- **Shared records**: reading to the end records whenever either `markNewComments` or `listNewPushCount` is on.
- **Known error**: when a moderator deletes comments, or the author edits comments away, the score counted in the article is lower than `.DIR`, so that article keeps showing a few extra points. It can only over-mark; it never sends keys.
- **Annotation layer**: on the article list (pageState 2), term_view asks `newComments.listBadge(board)` and gets `{ key, fn }`. `listNewPushKey` goes into `annotationsKey` (it changes whenever the stored records or the board change; article-list easy reading uses `rowIdentityStable`, and without the key, the list nodes would be reused unchanged on returning to the list after finishing an article). `listNewPushFor` looks each row up and puts the result in `listNewPush` (it reads only that row's own text, so rows stay independent).
- **Rendering**: native/desktop rows (`row.js`) overlay the badge on the last five cells only when blank, without extending the 80-column grid. Easy-reading article cards (`list_card.js`) place it on the second line, before the read mark and date. The right-hand group keeps its width; the number and author truncate first. The badge retains its original size despite the smaller metadata font. Text comes from `data-new-push` through `::after`, staying out of selection/copy. Cards without a badge retain their existing output.

## Tests

Unit: `list_new_push.test.js` (recommendation score, exact values of the list's number, matching rules, record format, listBadge's key and pref), `list_new_push_render.test.js` (real browser: overlay at the row's end without widening it, not drawn when it does not fit, position on cards), `new_comments.test.js` (counting, storing and reading records and the limit, recording only once read to the end, finding the row on reopening, switching articles, reverse reading, functionMode, pref off, how `applyNewCommentsMark` marks, `annotationsKey`). Offline e2e: `new_comments.offline.spec.js` (stock-huang: the first reading marks nothing and records 49 once read to the end; with 40 stored beforehand, the 41st comment has a line along its top edge that does not cover the row above, and jumping to new comments only scrolls the viewport and sends no keys; with no new comments nothing is marked).
