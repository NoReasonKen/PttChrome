# Image Viewer Integration

This records why the image viewer is integrated the way it is and how it connects to PttChrome's reading screen. The implementation uses PhotoSwipe **5.4.4**, whose core accepts an image data source without a React gallery and appends its dialog outside the article. That fits the existing plain DOM screen and avoids adding another React island. [PhotoSwipe 5.4.4 release](https://github.com/dimsemenov/PhotoSwipe/releases/tag/v5.4.4), [package exports](https://github.com/dimsemenov/PhotoSwipe/blob/v5.4.4/package.json), [MIT license](https://github.com/dimsemenov/PhotoSwipe/blob/v5.4.4/LICENSE), [data sources](https://photoswipe.com/data-sources/).

## Reading-screen boundary

`ScreenController` owns activation of a successfully loaded inline image. It dispatches the selected image URL, intrinsic dimensions, alt text, referrer policy, and trigger element to the application-owned `ImageViewer`. The viewer collects article images without moving the inline preview or replacing the article DOM. Passing loaded URLs preserves selected source candidates and avoids repeating host resolution. The PhotoSwipe image-load event copies each image's referrer policy to its element. Viewer image backgrounds and padding are explicitly cleared so global article image styles cannot add a white frame. [Screen activation](../src/render/screen.js), [gallery collection](../src/js/article_images.js), [viewer adapter](../src/js/image_viewer.js), [viewer styles](../src/css/image_viewer.css), [PhotoSwipe image loading](https://github.com/dimsemenov/PhotoSwipe/blob/v5.4.4/src/js/slide/content.js).

Mobile inline previews are capped at `50dvh` and their available column width; desktop previews retain the `23.75em` height and `39em` width caps. The viewer starts at PhotoSwipe's `fit` level, which fits the full image in the viewport without enlarging it beyond natural size. Double-tap toggles to exactly `fit * 2` and back to fit. Its maximum is `max(fit * 8, viewportWidth / naturalWidth * 2)`: the second term also lets a small or very tall image reach at least twice the viewport width. This is an explicit product limit; it can enlarge beyond both natural size and the viewport. Pinch continues from the current zoom and panning follows PhotoSwipe's bounds. [Preview styles](../src/css/main.css), [PhotoSwipe zoom options](https://photoswipe.com/adjusting-zoom-level/), [versioned zoom calculation](https://github.com/dimsemenov/PhotoSwipe/blob/v5.4.4/src/js/slide/zoom-level.js), [gesture behavior](https://github.com/dimsemenov/PhotoSwipe/blob/v5.4.4/src/js/gestures/zoom-handler.js).

## Gallery and asynchronous loading

`collectArticleImages` snapshots the article's image order, retaining duplicate link occurrences. Lazy slots expose their link through WeakMap metadata instead of forcing a mount. Cached album descriptions retain failed images as retryable entries and map successful candidates back to their original album positions. Videos, iframes, and ordinary unsupported links are skipped. Loaded images are immediately usable; unresolved links and unmeasured images become loading slides resolved only as PhotoSwipe visits their holders. `loadArticleImage` shares inline candidate order, two retries per candidate, backoff, and referer rules; its image requests and backoff are abortable. Shared metadata promises remain owned by the existing resolver cache. [Gallery module](../src/js/article_images.js), [slot metadata](../src/render/inline_preview_slot.js), [shared media policy](../src/components/ImagePreviewer.jsx).

The data source has empty boundary slides before and after its images. PhotoSwipe commits a native horizontal swipe to a boundary before the adapter closes, including articles containing only one image. Finger left means next, right means previous, and `loop: false` prevents wrapping. Dynamic album expansion mutates the data source and refreshes affected holders through PhotoSwipe's methods. Updates wait for dragging, pinching, and the slide spring to finish; refreshing neighbors preserves the selected image's zoom and pan. This prevents asynchronous metadata from reversing a committed swipe or resetting a reader's zoom. [Adapter](../src/js/image_viewer.js), [PhotoSwipe data-source methods](https://photoswipe.com/methods/), [gesture regression](../tests/unit/image_viewer_gallery.test.js).

## Loading scheduler assessment

The original scheduler proposal would maintain a fixed article-order viewer preload range of one previous image, the current image, and two following images, with at most four concurrent image loads. It also requested waiting for the current image before starting neighbors, updating the queue on navigation, and retaining completed images. This policy is **not implemented**. The existing viewer loads nearby images on demand and does not download every article image at startup. [Current activation and loading](../src/js/image_viewer.js), [gallery collection](../src/js/article_images.js).

Confirmed constraints for the future issue:

- Foreground image and swipe waiting time are the primary goals; reducing unnecessary requests is secondary. Preventing undocumented source-side throttling is not an acceptance guarantee.
- The four-load limit applies to viewer-owned image jobs, not all page traffic or a literal four HTTP requests. A job may require resolution, probes, fallback candidates, or retries. Inline preview scheduling remains outside the first version.

The feature remains deferred. Cancellation of already started jobs and whether foreground completion should block neighbor loading are still open design decisions.

The queue bookkeeping itself is small; no meaningful CPU cost is expected from managing four active jobs. This is a source-based estimate, not a device benchmark. Integration and regression coverage determine the cost:

| Requirement | Cost and constraint |
| --- | --- |
| Bound the viewer's own unresolved image jobs and prioritize the current entry | A small scheduler is feasible; it would need per-job cancellation and an explicit lifetime separate from deferred slide refresh. |
| Keep the fixed article-order window while navigating | Album resolution replaces one entry with several, so queue decisions must follow entry identity and recompute after expansion. PhotoSwipe's directional `preload` option alone does not express this policy. |
| Wait for the current image before starting neighbors | Downloads have no general deadline. A stalled image could block all preloading; failure and timeout policy would become part of the feature. |
| Guarantee a shared four-image network limit | This reaches beyond the viewer's jobs: dimension measurement uses `Image`, PhotoSwipe creates its own image element, and inline previews load independently. Cached bytes may be reused, but these are separate request lifetimes. |

The initially activated image must already have loaded inline, so strict current-first loading often adds little at entry. For later images it can trade faster foreground loading for slower subsequent navigation. PhotoSwipe provides cancelable loading hooks, so a package fork is unnecessary, but an asynchronous scheduler must explicitly resume those loads. Merely returning a Promise from an event handler does not defer the library's synchronous image loading. [Activation guard](../src/render/screen.js), [dimension loading](../src/js/article_images.js), [inline loading](../src/components/ImagePreviewer.jsx), [PhotoSwipe preload](https://photoswipe.com/options/#preload), [versioned content lifecycle](https://github.com/dimsemenov/PhotoSwipe/blob/v5.4.4/src/js/slide/content.js), [directional preload implementation](https://github.com/dimsemenov/PhotoSwipe/blob/v5.4.4/src/js/slide/loader.js).

Recommendation: defer the complete strict scheduler until measurements show a loading problem worth its moderate integration and maintenance cost. No measured slowdown or source-side throttling has been established by this assessment. Concurrency limits do not establish a host's request-rate or IP-throttling policy. If evidence justifies a smaller change, first bound only new viewer-owned jobs, prioritize the current entry, remove distant queued jobs, and cancel on close; keep inline loading and a strict current-first barrier outside that initial scope. Measure foreground load time, swipe waiting time, peak in-flight requests, and duplicate/retried requests before expanding it.

## Modal and close lifecycle

`App` owns the viewer and registers its close action with the existing sheet-dismissal priority, which connects Android system Back to the same close path. The viewer also closes when the article changes or the terminal settles on a different screen type, when the connection closes, or through its visible Close button and Escape. `setModalOpen('imageViewer', ...)` gates terminal input; while open, the viewer marks the rest of the document inert. The adapter aborts pending gallery work, restores prior inert values, unregisters dismissal, and releases the modal gate. [Application lifecycle and modal gate](../src/js/pttchrome.jsx), [viewer lifecycle](../src/js/image_viewer.js), [PhotoSwipe options](https://photoswipe.com/options/), [events](https://photoswipe.com/events/).

Closing the initially activated image preserves its existing article position. Closing after browsing centers the currently viewed image, using a fresh lookup after row rebuilds and rendering its chunk before measuring. Lazy mounting and image load can move that position, so mutation/resize/load observers recenter on the next animation frame for at most ten seconds. User touch, wheel, or keyboard input cancels this settling immediately, as do a different article, another modal, or a new viewer. This operation sends no PTT keys or screen-navigation commands and therefore needs no reading-record transaction. [Position restoration](../src/js/image_viewer.js), [chunk activation](../src/render/screen_chunk.js), [reading-record scope](reading-record.md).

The adapter keeps PhotoSwipe-specific options in `src/js/image_viewer.js`. PhotoSwipe supplies gesture recognition, zoom levels, panning, focus containment, and Escape handling; PttChrome supplies article collection, modal ownership, system-back integration, and article position restoration. This division keeps touch behavior in the image viewer and screen ownership in the application.

Keep the modal gate active until PhotoSwipe emits `destroy`: even with opening/closing animations disabled, `close()` can complete on a later timer. The adapter reports `imageviewerchange` on `window`; the history guard uses this lifetime to close the viewer before terminal navigation. When terminal Back mapping is disabled, it creates and retires only the temporary viewer history entry. A Back-triggered close must not restore that entry while destruction is pending. Programmatic history cleanup also mutes deep-link consumption through the existing self-navigation scope. [Viewer lifecycle](../src/js/image_viewer.js), [history ownership](../src/js/history_back_guard.js), [asynchronous close regression](../tests/unit/history_back_guard.test.js).
