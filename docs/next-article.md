# Next article after the end (`next_article.js`)

Read this before touching `next_article.js` or the CSS of the title card at the article's end (`#nextArticleCard` in `css/main.css`).

## What it is

When an easy-reading article is read to its end, a gap is left below the last comment, followed by a 「下一篇」 ("next article") title card (title, comment count, author and date, taken from the list snapshot). Tapping it switches to that article; on desktop the reader can also pause at the end and keep scrolling down, and on mobile pull up once more. **Scrolling to the bottom never switches articles by itself** (L 10/19: the reader might miss the comments at the very bottom; reconfirmed by L 10/21: each article must stay a separate reading). It has no switch of its own: it sends keys on the reader's behalf, so it runs whenever easy reading is on.

## Rules

- **The next article is the next row in list order**: the native "space after finishing" is pmore `pmore_cmd_space` → `READ_NEXT` → read.c `read_key_handle_mode_navigation`'s `crs_ln + 1`, not "the next new article": after the last article come the pinned articles, and within search results it is the next result. So the card trusts only the row in the list snapshot (`split_reader.listPageOf`/`mergeSnapshot`, kept separately and independent of the split-reading switch) whose number is exactly "this article + 1". Rows that article-list easy reading hides (deleted, locked, blacklisted) are skipped, and the next number is tried; the criterion is `list_row_filter#hiddenReason` (see `docs/list-row-filter.md`), with the blacklist as configured at the moment the end was reached.
- **No way on means an end card, not no card** (L 10/21: with nothing there, the reader would think the feature broke). When this article is recognised but no next article can be found, the card turns into a dashed, non-clickable frame (`data-end`, `aria-disabled`):
  - `last`, 「已無下一篇」 ("no next article"): the list ends after this article. How the end is recognised (`split_reader#listPageOf`'s `endNum`): read.c's list is a scrolling window (`read_move_cursor`'s base); the pinned articles (bottom_count) always sort after every numbered article, and rows past last_line are drawn blank, so the last numbered row followed directly by pinned articles or blank rows is the last article. When numbered articles fill the screen down to its bottom row, the end cannot be seen (`null`). The snapshot remembers ends it has seen and discards them once a larger number (a new article) appears (`mergeSnapshot`). `listEndsAfter`: true only when every number from the next one to the end has been seen (and all were skipped).
  - `unknown`, 「已到文末」 ("end of article"): the next number is on a list page not yet seen (most commonly, this article is the bottom row of its list page). The card asks the reader to go back to the list and page down; once they have, the next article appears here.
  - When this article is not recognised (a deep link, no list seen, another board…), no card appears at all.
- **Search results and digests keep a separate snapshot**: MODE_SELECT renumbers the list (read.c's remap), and native `READ_NEXT` within results is the next result, so for an article opened from search results the card takes the next number from the results snapshot (`_subSnapshot`). The screen does not show the search criteria, so as soon as any number on a new page disagrees with the old snapshot (author plus subject; deleted rows do not count), the whole snapshot is replaced (`mergeSubSnapshot`); seeing the full list (having left the search) also drops it. The full list's snapshot is stored separately, and each uses its own numbering.
- **Unrecognised means native**: the card appears only for "an easy-reading article (pageState 3, `_enabled`, not in functionMode, not in reverse reading) + read to its end (`easyReadingReachedPageEnd`) + the last list seen was a list of the same board (full list or search results) + this article is recognised in that snapshot (`findCurrentNum`)". It does not appear for an article opened directly from a deep link.
- **For an article whose title was changed, the list differs from the header**: PTT's `T` (bbs.c `edit_title`) changes only `.DIR` (`modify_dir_lite`) and leaves the article file's header alone, so the subject never matches. So when the subject does not match, "which article is this" falls back to "the article the cursor stood on before it opened", trusted when the author matches (`findCurrentNum`'s hintFresh). The cursor is trustworthy only for **the first article after the list was seen** (`split_reader.cursorHintFresh`: the first header seen is recorded on `_lastList`; a different header afterwards means the reader switched articles with native keys inside the article, and the cursor stopped being that article long ago). It must be recorded on every article settle, not only when the end is reached. The landing check works the same way: when the header's subject does not match, it compares the row the cursor stopped on after `<number>⏎` during the switch (`_nav.seen`, list against list).
- **Switching uses native keys**: for the full list, `aid_navigation.startNum(board, number)` (inside the article, `s<board>⏎` → `<number>⏎`, verifying the cursor → `⏎`, verifying the landing is an article). For search results, `startSubNum(board, number, verify)`: `s<board>` and `#` both leave the results or are refused, so it presses `←` inside the article instead (pmore exits back to i_read, still in the same results; it requires landing on a sub-list of the same board) → `<number>⏎` (read_cmd_num looks only at the current list) → **`⏎` only when the cursor row's author and subject are the article on the card** (the snapshot may come from an earlier search; when unrecognised, it stops on the list rather than opening the wrong article). It only starts from a pager proven to be READING. After landing, author and subject are compared once more with the article on the card (`landedOn`); on a mismatch (the list changed while we were not looking) an error-level notice is shown, no further key is sent, and the reader stays on the article PTT gave. Reading record `next-article`: never restored (as `split-open`). On Back to the list with `←`, the list's selection is still the article originally opened, and PTT's cursor also jumps back to it by number (#123, see `docs/easy-reading-list.md` N6c).
- **Never writes `#mainContainer`**: the card is a `<button>` inside `.main` (easy reading's scroll container), after `#screenRoot`, and scrolls along with the article's end; the core rendering chain does not know it exists. It is created the first time it has to show, and afterwards only `hidden` is toggled.
- **The card's position: right below the last row of content, and above the footer and the comment bar when scrolled to the bottom**. `#mainContainer` ends with blank space: for an article with few comments, pmore draws the remaining rows blank; easy reading's footer has `paddingBottom`; the bottom comment bar adds an `::after` (`html.commentBarShown`). Placed simply after all that, the card would need a scroll to be seen on articles with few comments; and with those two reserved gaps *before* the card, the fixed comment bar would cover the card at the bottom. So `_layout` measures two facts and writes them as CSS variables, and `main.css #nextArticleCard` computes the margins: `--next-pull-up` = the trailing blank space (`trailingBlankPx`: measured from the bottom edge of the last node with content to the bottom edge of `#mainContainer`; a blank row is one with only whitespace, no image, and every background `b0`, so a blank row with a background colour counts as content; `position: fixed`/`absolute` nodes do not count: foreground tools live in `#screenTools`, but standalone renderers without an external tools host still mount `.floatTools` beside rows; counting these overlays would pull the card into the middle of the article), which makes the top margin negative so the card moves up over the blank space; `--next-bottom-inset` = how much of `.main`'s bottom is covered (the top of the footer, the top of the comment bar as computed by `state.bottom`, `view.keyboardInset` = mobile bottom navigation bar plus soft keyboard, whichever is highest; on mobile `.main` extends a few px behind the navigation bar, so adding only the fixed bar height is off by those few px), which the bottom margin leaves free. This whole class of "an overlay covers the article's end" is guarded generically by the e2e `bottom_overlay`/`mobile_bottom_overlay` feature-combination matrix (`tests/e2e/helpers/overlay_guard.js`). When it re-measures: when the card shows, the frame after each settle (content changed without the height changing), `#mainContainer`'s ResizeObserver (page fills, previews, font size, and the comment bar's `::after`, which a React effect adds a step later), and the comment bar's `onChange`. When the whole card shrinks into the blank space it takes no scroll height, so `trailingPx` has a lower bound of 0.
- **No native flash while switching: the veil** (L 10/21). A switch really travels through native keys, and `aid_navigation._begin` puts easy reading in functionMode to mirror native, so without a cover the native article page and the list would flash by. `#nextArticleVeil` is an opaque layer under body, `position: fixed` and aligned to `.main`'s rectangle, put up **before `startNum`/`startSubNum`** (start enters functionMode synchronously; covering first means no frame leaks out). On it sits the next article's title, sliding from the card's current position to the top (like scrolling the next article up; no slide under `prefers-reduced-motion`). Taking it down: on failure, or when start does not take the request, it is removed at once (the reader must see the native screen PTT gave and the error notice; unrecognised means native); on success, it fades out one frame after easy reading has taken over the new article (`pageState 3`, `_enabled`, not in functionMode, checked on every settle), waiting at most 2.5 seconds. It intercepts no rendering and never touches `#mainContainer`; it only covers it. Like the card, mouse events do not propagate to window (`isNextArticleTarget` recognises both). **This is not infinite scrolling**: the new article is still a separate page, read from the top.
- **"Scrolled to the end yet" must subtract the card**: `reading_record.trailingPx(view)` (the height of `view.nextArticleCard` plus its top and bottom margins). A reader resting on the last comment, with the card still below the viewport, counts as at the end (returning to the same place after a disconnect, and refresh's "stick to the end", rely on it).
- **Mouse**: the App's mouse entry point is on window, so the card itself calls preventDefault on `mousedown` (so it does not take focus from `#t`) and stopPropagation on all three events; `App._onUploadLayer` recognises `#nextArticleCard` (the wheel still scrolls `.main` and does not become a terminal click).
- **Design**: thin neutral-grey border and small text, no colour (the comment area already has OP blue, own-comment green and floor yellow, and L asked for the comment area not to get gaudy).
- **Nothing is sent while the line is busy**: when `serializedOpHint` has a value or the CommandQueue is not idle, nothing is sent; while switching, the card shows 「開啟中…」 ("opening…"), with a 45-second watchdog.
- The card is an easy-reading behaviour with no switch of its own: every easy-reading spec has it at the end of the accumulated page.

## Pull past the end: pull up on mobile, pause and scroll on on desktop (`pull_next_article.js`)

The version L agreed to is "tap the title card **or** pull past a threshold", and **reaching the bottom must never switch articles**. On mobile, "scrolling down" is the finger pushing up, so there it is called "pulling up". Both entry points only change the card's text and the progress bar under it (`next_article.setPull(pull, progress)`; the card gets `data-pull` and the CSS variable `--next-pull`); an end card does not take them.

### Mobile: at the bottom, pull up (`installPullNextArticle`)

- **Why touch events**: this is the same exception as Pull-to-refresh (`docs/screen-refresh.md`). The browser's overscroll gives the page no way to learn that "the user pulled further at the bottom", while touch events have full phases (finger still down / released), so "takes effect on release past the threshold" and "pulling back cancels" are possible. Listening is passive (`passive` plus capture) and never calls `preventDefault`, so scrolling, Android's native overscroll stretch and taps all behave as before; we only decide at release whether to open.
- **Start conditions**: mobile layout, not in selection mode, no modal, a single finger, the card showing (there is a next article) and not already opening, and the `.main` under the touch point **already scrolled to the bottom** (`atScrollBottom`; content shorter than the viewport also counts). Starting on the toolbar, App Bar, a sheet, a dialog, an input or the card itself does not count (tapping the card goes through click).
- **Threshold**: it reuses Pull-to-refresh's `pullState` directly (damping 0.5, threshold 64px, diagonal pulls cancel), with the direction mirrored. While pulling, the card's text follows: before the threshold 「下一篇・再往上拉」 ("next article · pull up more"), past it 「放開開啟下一篇」 ("release to open the next article"), and the progress bar = offset / threshold. On release it calls `open()` only past the threshold; not reaching it, pulling back, a diagonal pull, or scrolling away from the bottom midway only restores the text.

### Desktop: scroll to the bottom, pause, scroll on (`installWheelNextArticle`)

- **The difficulty**: WheelEvent has no phases. The stream of wheel events that scrolls all the way to the bottom, and a touchpad's inertia tail, keep sending wheel events after the bottom is reached, so simply accumulating "offset after the bottom" would mean reaching the bottom switches articles.
- **Split by gesture** (pure function `wheelPullStep`): a wheel event only starts a new gesture when it comes `WHEEL_GESTURE_GAP_MS` (250ms) or more after the previous one (inertia and continuous wheel turns have far shorter gaps). **Only a gesture that starts while already resting at the bottom** accumulates its downward offset, and past `WHEEL_THRESHOLD_PX` (480px, about 4 to 5 mouse notches) it calls `open()`. The stream that scrolls to the bottom did not start at the bottom, so all of it (inertia included) does not count; the reader has to stop and scroll again. Scrolling up, or leaving the bottom midway, resets to zero; `WHEEL_IDLE_RESET_MS` (700ms) without activity resets to zero and restores the card's text. Slow notch-by-notch scrolling (250 to 700ms between notches) still accumulates.
- **"Started at the bottom" must exclude the compositor scrolling first**: by the time a passive wheel listener runs, the compositor may already have applied that notch (measured, `tests/e2e/README.md`), so looking only at `atScrollBottom` would treat "the notch that reached the bottom" as starting at the bottom. So a capture listener on `.main`'s scroll records the last resting position, and a notch before which the position already changed (`moved`) does not count.
- The listener is on window, capture, passive, and never calls `preventDefault`: the browser still does the scrolling. Only the `.main` under the pointer counts (the wheel over split reading's left pane or the article tool sidebar does not); it does not engage with a modal open, with Ctrl + wheel (zoom), when horizontal movement dominates (`swipe_gesture#isHorizontalWheel`), or during an AID jump.
- When the card does not appear (easy reading off), neither gesture exists.
